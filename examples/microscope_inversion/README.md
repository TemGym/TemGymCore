# The NEOARM through PyJEM: recording, and fitting TemGym to the recordings

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| [NEOARM forward model](neoarm_forward.ipynb) | Start here. Trace a console readback to the SightX, compare the column with real CLA1 kicks, lens wobbles and condenser settings, then work through the diffraction-ring and grating scale calibration. |
| [NEOARM predictor](neoarm_predictor.ipynb) | Rehearse acquisition through the offline PyJEM interface, fit the column from recorded frames and DACs, and check its pixel predictions on reserved settings. The notebook includes the microscope recording recipe. From this directory, run `python at_the_microscope/record_predictor.py --simulated pilot --compact` to repeat the compact rehearsal. |
| [`at_the_microscope/`](at_the_microscope/) | Copy this folder to the microscope PC (needs numpy and PyJEM only). |
| [`at_the_microscope/neoarm.py`](at_the_microscope/neoarm.py) | Shared by every script: connect (real or simulated), `ask` (read), `change` (set; only printed in a dry run), `set_lenses`, `set_deflector`, `take_frame`, `find_beam`, `record` (a frame and every lens and deflector value: one row), `restore`, `save`. For a second camera: `add_camera`, `use_camera`; for the rulers: `find_rings`, `find_pitch`. |
| [`at_the_microscope/record_console_tables.py`](at_the_microscope/record_console_tables.py) | A plain PyJEM script, no beam (blanked): every selector row in MAG, LowMAG, SAMAG and DIFF, and every spot at every alpha at the row the console is on; at each it reads every lens, every deflector pair, HT and the stage. Puts the console back and saves `console_tables.json`. Run it before the lens relax. |
| [`at_the_microscope/record_projection_response.py`](at_the_microscope/record_projection_response.py) | How far CLA1 (MAG 40k) and CLA2 (DIFF 40 cm) displace the beam, with IL1, IL2, IL3, PL1 each changed up and down. |
| [`at_the_microscope/beam_per_magnification.py`](at_the_microscope/beam_per_magnification.py) | At every MAG row from 8k to 500k: size the beam with CL3, centre it with CLA1, and measure px per CLA1 unit. |
| [`at_the_microscope/record_predictor.py`](at_the_microscope/record_predictor.py) | One recording, five stages, no specimen: every lens value at every row and spot (`survey`); the same lens values reached from below and above (`history`); CLA1 and CLA2 kicks at every magnification row and camera length, and with IL1-PL1 changed at a few rows (`kicks`); tests of the deflectors (`deflectors`); the beam in MAG and DIFF over spot size and CL3 (`condenser`). Name stages to run only those; `--quick` is a short version; `--holes` also changes the condenser aperture. |
| [`at_the_microscope/record_two_cameras.py`](at_the_microscope/record_two_cameras.py) | One recording on both cameras (the screen camera and the Merlin), 186 frames, in cells like `record_projection_response.py` (run it whole or cell by cell; `--simulated`, nothing for a dry run, `--for-real`). No weak lenses (they showed no beam on the real NEOARM): six normal rows on the screen camera; at MAG 8000 and DIFF 40 cm only PL1 is turned down (40000, 24000, 12000) with CLA1 and CLA2 kicks on both cameras; gold rings and a grating image; `--kept` adds eight test rows. The camera is changed by the script: it moves the screen with PyJEM (`getScreen` / `setScreen`: 1 the SightX, 2 the Merlin) and waits 4 s; `--by-hand` asks you to do it instead. |
| [`at_the_microscope/merlin_camera.py`](at_the_microscope/merlin_camera.py), [`merlin_server.py`](at_the_microscope/merlin_server.py) | Only needed if PyJEM does not list the Merlin as one of its cameras. `merlin_server.py` runs on the Merlin PC: it answers a GET request (`/frame`) by running one line you fill in (`read_frame()`) and sending back the NumPy array; `/ping` checks the connection; `--fake` gives made-up frames for trying the network. `merlin_camera.py` is the microscope PC side: `Merlin().take_frame()` sends that GET and returns the array (set `MERLIN_URL` in it). |
| [`recordings/`](recordings/) | Recordings as copied from the microscope PC, one folder per day (zips, not in git). |
| [`fit_projection_response.ipynb`](fit_projection_response.ipynb) | Fits TemGym `ElectromagneticLens`es (IL1-PL1) to a projection-response recording, tests the fit on settings it never saw and on the magnification recording, shows the frames, and writes a virtual NEOARM from the fit to `results/` (the one used for rehearsals is designed: see `../virtual_neoarm/`). |
| [`fit_predictor.ipynb`](fit_predictor.ipynb) | Builds a predictor from the latest `record_predictor.py` recording: how well a displacement repeats, the lag of each lens by the route to its value, the deflector tests, a TemGym column fitted to half the rows and tested on the other half and on settings recorded afterwards, and the beam's size and spread of angles against spot and CL3. All in DAC units and pixels. |
| [`two_cameras_design.ipynb`](two_cameras_design.ipynb) | A design study, before any recording, for the two cameras at two heights (the screen camera and the Merlin). TemGym only, no PyJEM and no images. Shows what the second camera adds, the experiment built on it (one lens at a time with the others weak, then normal rows, gold rings and a grating image), that it finds the lens places and the camera distance in metres, how many frames it takes, the shift and tilt offsets, why many magnifications and camera lengths alone are not enough, the smallest recording that still works (the recipe of `record_two_cameras.py`), and, as an extra, what it says about the condenser. |
| [`fit_two_cameras.ipynb`](fit_two_cameras.ipynb) | Fits the latest `record_two_cameras.py` recording: a TemGym column from the specimen to both cameras with nothing held. Gives where each projection lens is and how strong, the distance between the cameras, the size of a CLA1 and a CLA2 unit (all in real units, through the two rulers), the misses on the kept-back rows, and from the undeflected beam the lens centres, the Merlin's middle and the beam's shift and tilt at the specimen. |
| [`condenser_design.ipynb`](condenser_design.ipynb) | A design study for fitting the condenser (CL1, CL2, CL3, an unknown source, the aperture holes) from the beam's size, spread of angles, brightness and position at the specimen. TemGym only. A ladder of about 50 condenser settings (about 100 frames) gives a condenser that predicts the beam to about 1 %; it pins CL3 and the holes but not where CL1, CL2 and the source are, and says why. |
| [`neoarm_model.ipynb`](neoarm_model.ipynb) | The whole NEOARM as one non-dimensional TemGym column (lengths in units of L, lens excitation as the image rotation θ, lens strength L/f = θ² φ / w with w the field's effective length): one table of numbers and two functions, console DAC values in, the beam's centre and size on the camera out. Also: the fit of the projection lenses to the console's lens table (the family of seven columns, `results/neoarm_family.json`), a console row, the slopes JAX gives for every control, the beam's size against CL3, three tests against the real NEOARM (CLA1 at every magnification from 40k alone, projection lenses changed one at a time, the parallel beam per spot, which also fits CL1 and CL3), and what frames from one camera cannot tell. Saves `results/neoarm_model.json`. |
| [`neoarm_predictor_offline.ipynb`](neoarm_predictor_offline.ipynb) | The rehearsal of the predictor session without the microscope: a hidden NEOARM-like column (another member of `results/neoarm_family.json`, with hidden misalignments, deflector strengths and its own L) behind the simulated PyJEM, driven by the real console tables; a recording made with plain PyJEM calls (size and centre the beam, kick every deflector, wobble every projection lens, at 21 rows); the model fitted to it with JAX from 240 starts; the test on kept-back rows, a kept-back spot and moves of several controls at once; steering with the fitted model; which frames are needed; and why lens positions and L need rulers, PL1 steps or the second camera. |
| [`results/`](results/) | What the notebooks save: the fitted models. |

Each script runs three ways, from `at_the_microscope/`:

    python record_projection_response.py --simulated     # the virtual NEOARM (needs TemGym)
    python record_projection_response.py                 # the real NEOARM, a dry run: reads it, changes nothing
    python record_projection_response.py --for-real      # the real NEOARM

A recording goes to `at_the_microscope/output/<name>/<date_time>/`: `table.csv` (one row per frame, the same columns
from every script), `console.json` (what the console had), `frames/`, and a copy of the scripts used.

A new script: start from `Neoarm("<name>", for_real=..., simulated=...)`, keep every change to the microscope inside
`scope.change`, `scope.set_lenses` and `scope.set_deflector`, and save every frame worth keeping with `scope.record(...)`.
